Privacy Policy

What this website, the AxePower desktop app and the AxePower phone app do with your data.

Last updated: 6 October 2026

1. This website

If you allow it, this website uses Google Analytics to understand how many people visit and which pages they read. Nothing is loaded and no analytics cookies are set until you choose “Accept” in the cookie banner. If you accept, Google Analytics sets cookies in your browser and collects information such as the pages you view, how you arrived, your browser and device type, and an approximate location derived from your IP address. Advertising features are turned off. This information is processed by Google under its own privacy policy; we use it only in aggregate to improve the site, and never to identify you or for advertising.

You can change your choice at any time with Cookie settings (also in every page's footer); choosing “Reject” removes the analytics cookies. If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal, analytics stays off unless you turn it on yourself. Your choice is stored only in your browser. The site works the same either way.

Separately from analytics, the company that hosts this website keeps standard server logs, such as your IP address, the page requested and the time, to deliver the site and protect it from abuse. We do not use these logs to identify visitors.

Links to downloads, source code, community spaces and donation services take you to other websites, which have their own privacy policies. If you donate, the payment service you choose handles the payment and shares only what it tells you it shares with the recipient.

2. The AxePower desktop app

The app has no advertising and no analytics or usage tracking. Accounts are optional and everything works without one. Without an account, it sends nothing about you or how you use it to its developers. With one, only what section 3 lists is stored, and none of it is sold or shared for advertising.

Stored on your computer: your settings, power profiles, themes and automations; music data such as library folders, playlists, recently played songs, recent searches and the saved queue; diagnostics logs, with tokens, links, email addresses and your user and computer names removed, kept for 14 days or 20 MB; a history of the changes AxePower made to Windows, so they can be rolled back; backups that some tools make before changing your system; and, if you sign in, your sign-in, encrypted so only your Windows user can read it. These are kept in the app-data folders of your Windows user profile and never leave your computer on their own. Deleting those folders removes them.

Online features. Some features contact other services directly from your computer. One happens on its own: while “Check for updates automatically” is on (it is by default), AxePower asks GitHub for a newer version when it starts. Everything else happens only when you use that feature:

  • signing in, your profile and Friends (online status, friend requests, invites), with Supabase, only if you have an account;
  • searching and streaming music on YouTube, which sends your search text and the song being played;
  • finding lyrics (LRCLIB, KuGou), which sends the song's title, artist, album and length;
  • posters for your installed games, from Steam's image servers;
  • pings: to 1.1.1.1 (Cloudflare) every 5 seconds while the Dashboard is on screen, for its latency reading, and to four public DNS servers when you run the DNS benchmark;
  • downloading AxePower updates, graphics drivers (NVIDIA, AMD), the Share Screen tools and portable apps (GitHub and the publishers' sites), and installing software through WinGet, when you ask;
  • links, downloads and help pages, which open in your web browser.

The app's Trust & Permissions page lists every one of these connections, what triggers it and what it sends, together with the ports it opens and where it keeps data. Remote control of the PC from the phone app over the internet is turned off in this version.

Like any website, these services also see your IP address and the request itself, and their own privacy policies and terms apply to what they receive. AxePower does not route this traffic through any server of its own.

Your devices and connections. Phone control, file transfer, remote playback control, calls and screen sharing work over your local network or an address you choose to connect to, and data goes directly between those devices, not through AxePower's servers. Local-network access is protected by a private link shared by QR code, and nothing is shared until you start it. When you invite a friend through your account, the invite carries your room's link to that friend only.

System access. By default the app runs with administrator rights (a setting you can turn off) and starts at sign-in through a scheduled task, so it can change power, performance and system settings. It changes your system only when you choose an action or set up an automation. Before a change it shows what will change and whether it is fully, partly or not reversible; changes it can undo are listed in Settings → Change history. Third-party uninstallers and debloat scripts cannot be undone by AxePower, and their confirmations say so.

3. Your AxePower account (optional)

If you create an account in the desktop app, these are stored by Supabase, the service that runs AxePower accounts:

  • how you sign in: your email address, or the Google, Discord, GitHub or Steam account you use (its ID, name and email if it has one). Passwords are kept only as a secure hash;
  • your profile: @username, display name, the link to your profile picture from that service, and when you joined;
  • friends: your friends and friend requests, the people you blocked, and invites you send, which expire after 10 minutes;
  • while the app is open and you're signed in: whether you're online or away and, unless you turn it off, what you're doing (the game you're playing, in a call, sharing your screen).

Who can see it. Anyone signed in to AxePower can see your @username, display name and picture, so friends can find you. Your friends list, status, activity and invites are visible only to you and the friends involved. Settings → Friends lets you hide your activity or appear offline.

When you sign in with Google, Discord, GitHub or Steam, that service's own privacy policy covers the sign-in. For Steam, AxePower also looks up your public Steam name and picture.

Deleting it. Deleting your account on the app's Profile page removes your account, profile, friends, status and invites. The services involved may keep short-lived technical logs under their own policies.

4. The AxePower phone app

The phone app talks only to your own PC, over your local Wi-Fi or a USB connection. It does not use AxePower accounts yet, has no analytics, and has no connection to any cloud service.

5. Open-source components and third-party tools

AxePower is built with open-source libraries and can install, launch or work alongside third-party tools. Each remains the property of its authors and is used under its own license; the full license texts are included with the app in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md, next to AxePower.exe. Any tool you launch through AxePower is covered by that tool's own terms and privacy policy. AxePower is an independent project and is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by any of them. All product names, logos and trademarks belong to their respective owners.

6. Children

AxePower and this website are not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has created an AxePower account, contact us and we will delete it.

7. For authors and rights holders

If you are the author or owner of any software, content or trademark that AxePower uses or refers to, and you would like a credit added, corrected or removed, please contact us or open an issue on GitHub. We respect creators' rights and will act on such requests promptly.

8. Changes and contact

If the way AxePower or this website handles data changes, this page will be updated and the date at the top will change. Questions about this policy can be raised through the project's GitHub issues.
